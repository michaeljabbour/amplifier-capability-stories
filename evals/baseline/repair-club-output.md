# A shorter first step at the repair club

Fictional development example. Drafts only; nothing was published or sent.

## Case study

The repair club tried a one-page booking form for three Saturdays after replacing its long form on September 5, 2026. The limited trial gives the project team a useful question to investigate, rather than proof of a successful rollout. [S1]

The log records 42 visits across those Saturdays: 12, 16 and 14. It does not identify unique people or completed repairs. Six voluntary feedback cards offer a small view of the experience: four called the first step clearer, while two asked for an example photo. There was no baseline feedback survey. [S2, S3]

Those records support continuing to examine the first step. They do not establish an attendance effect, faster repairs, saved volunteer time or financial return. Comparable waiting-time observations are a proposed next check, not an outcome of this trial. [S4]

## Executive adaptation

The booking-page trial has an encouraging but limited usability signal: four of six voluntary feedback cards called the first step clearer; two requested an example photo. It ran for three Saturdays and logged 42 visits, not a count of unique people or completed repairs. [S1–S3]

Keep the next decision narrow. Consider a further trial with comparable waiting-time observations. Current records contain no waiting-time, staff-time, attendance-effect or financial-return measurement, so a claim of 50% time saved or positive ROI would be unsupported. [S4]
